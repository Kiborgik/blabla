# 0.10 product goal feedback and acceptance plan

**Recommendation: HOLD product-readiness acceptance.** The candidate has useful engineering foundations and verified package/CI receipts. Its product purpose needs a clear demonstration: a development goal leads to an inexpensive observation, that observation changes the next action, and the resulting work is reviewed against the original goal. This document revises the acceptance direction and records one owner-approved goal-guidance correction. It does not authorize release or broader product changes.

## Purpose

BlaBla should make useful progress easier for a coding agent to recognize and pursue. That applies to every kind of development goal: correctness, authored content, appearance, usability, maintainability and the experience the owner wants to create. A signal earns its place by pointing to a next action or a justified success, at a cost small enough to use during ordinary work.

“Cheap dopamine” is a metaphor for this behavioral steering: give the agent frequent, specific feedback for making the right thing better. It makes no claim about an agent's literal feelings. A growing test count, completed task record or green badge is a poor reward when it can increase while the desired experience remains absent.

The concrete loop is **goal → observable evidence → correction → reviewed outcome**. Queryable intent keeps the target available; deterministic checks catch the obligations they actually reach; questions and findings preserve uncertainty; current evidence and review prevent an old success from approving changed work. The owner still chooses the goal and accepts judgments the tool cannot establish.

## What exists and what 0.10 changes

| Part of the loop | Available capability | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recover intent | Mission, System, Process, Knowledge and Goals; `status` and `explain` | Relevant declared context, not automatic agreement between prose and implementation |
| Ask a useful question | Task questions, decisions, findings and lens assessments, already present before 0.10 | A recorded question/pick/assessment and its required workflow, not the quality of the judgment |
| Observe behavior or structure | Executable contracts, finite campaigns, static facts, witnesses and counterexamples | The declared obligations reached by those mechanisms |
| Check a content or quality slice | An exact task check over declared inputs, plus inspection and independent review | The check's observed result and the reviewer’s bounded assessment; coverage must be designed |
| Keep feedback current | 0.10 exact-check/input/epoch binding and revision-bound lens/review credit | Old evidence cannot approve relevant changed work through that recorded handoff |
| Divide work safely | 0.10 disjoint live write scopes and withdrawal/residue accounting | Recorded ownership and unresolved changes, not host-level prevention of arbitrary file edits |
| Evaluate possible expert advice | 0.10 typed judgments, selected packets, policy, replay and calibration | Experimental advice infrastructure; useful live advice remains unqualified |

The [0.9 changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md#090-2026-09-24) already records goals and questions. The [0.10 changes](../../CHANGELOG.md#0100-unreleased) improve the reliability of the loop; they did not introduce all of its concepts.

Goal `expect` entries name rule/contract identities, not arbitrary images, prose judgments or check results. Nonformal goals can guide task statements, questions and review, but the current goal evaluator has no general semantic acceptance mechanism for them. Goals, tasks, memory and experts never change `OVERALL`; only the active Behavior and Structure layers decide canonical completion. See [goal semantics](../project.md#goals) and [completion](../project.md#layers-and-completion).

## Worked example from a creature game

A private, unfinished game expansion supplied this example. It is anonymized to protect unpublished source and artwork. These observations are a development account reviewed on 2 October 2026, not a public reproducibility package or a controlled comparison.

The owner wanted richer, visibly distinct playable content. Reporting more tests did not answer that request. The owner explicitly redirected the work toward exact playable additions, real rendered art and meaningful environment consequences.

| Product question | Observation available | Limit or remaining acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are all six added species genuinely playable? | Six pinned ordinary founder-selection → scouting → deployment → real first-fight → settled save/replay journeys reached matching replay state hashes without a simulation fault | First fights do not establish whole-campaign balance, every synergy or successor/NG+ behavior |
| Does each species look distinct in the game’s visual language? | Exact identity-to-art bindings and a contact sheet produced by the shared portrait renderer, including full-size, small portraits and color-free silhouettes, were inspected | A binding check cannot judge appeal; a contact sheet does not establish six-actor combat readability or physical-device performance |
| Do the new environments change preparation rather than just colors? | Real shortest-path/preparation witnesses rejected an initial tunnel-route design and an initial contact topology. Corrections used plain side routes and shared contact positions rather than weakening the witnesses | Integrated play and visual acceptance remain separate; different graph counts or hashes alone would not establish a meaningful choice |
| Do content slices work together? | Real first-fight checks exposed missing provenance for preplaced terrain markers; the responsible slice repaired it and the same journeys then passed | A scoped success does not replace final integrated acceptance |

**Attribution matters.** The owner supplied the product correction; the planner and reviewers turned it into exact acceptance questions; ordinary checks and rendered artifacts exposed the observations. BlaBla recorded bounded tasks, questions, findings and evidence, and enforced recorded scope/freshness prerequisites. There is no evidence that the expert layer originated the correction, understood the artwork or reduced total development effort.

The useful feedback was specific: “this route does not produce the promised contact,” followed by an observable correction. That is the kind of success signal BlaBla should make cheap to preserve and act on. Counting the tests added while making that correction would obscure the result.

## The goal signal gap and focused correction

The candidate provides its own counterexample. At commit `db29cf7`, `status` reported all five expectations held for `goal::expert-loop-demonstrated-in-real-work` and suggested setting it to done. Its [statement](../../goals.bla) calls for actual host delivery, acknowledgment versus observed correction, and matched off/shadow/advisory work. Its expectations name five deterministic contracts that can all hold without establishing those outcomes.

This follows the current [goal evaluator](../../src/memory/goal.rs): readiness depends on an active state and all declared expectations holding. The [goal tests](../../tests/goals.rs) intentionally cover that behavior. The goal remains active, and its prose explicitly says the contracts are necessary checks rather than proof of live benefit. The gap is between the machine's suggested next action and the broader product acceptance, not a newly demonstrated failure of contract evaluation.

The owner approved a narrow correction: `status` now routes an all-held active goal to outcome review instead of instructing the owner to mark it done. `explain goal` says that broader outcome acceptance is not established by the contract checks, and the authority text identifies the state as owner-declared. The internal output helper is named for review; stored goal states and evidence, JSON structure, expectation verdicts and `OVERALL`/exit-code semantics are unchanged.

The regression uses a goal whose contracts hold while its statement requires still-unverified live results. It checks the next-action route and explanation, confirms the goal file is not changed by observation, and preserves structured verdict/completion results. A second regression covers active, done and dropped states without inventing an observed signoff. Both failed against the old output before the correction.

This deliberately implements no persisted outcome-signoff mechanism and infers no truth from prose. A historical `done` remains the author's declaration, not newly manufactured proof of review. Contract readiness, broader acceptance and expert qualification remain separate; adding a nonformal goal still does not make `OVERALL` reject it.

## Task ceremony audit

The workflow should buy more useful feedback than it costs. These are observed mechanics and bounded improvement options, not measured speedup claims:

- **Acceptance and rework:** `accept → evidence → lens → challenge → ready` preserves a reviewable handoff. Reacceptance starts a new epoch, so relevant checks and assessments must be renewed. Use one coherent scoped slice rather than opening a task for every tiny edit; do not bypass freshness to save commands
- **Dependency declaration:** `task check` replaces its input list. Release preparation had to restore the full list after changing the check. Keep the exact inputs visible and repeat them deliberately; the tool does not discover all dependencies
- **Review timing:** inspect a stable slice and its whole diff, then renew review only when its relevant target changes. Repeatedly reviewing moving shared work creates churn; unrelated changes already preserve credit
- **Verification placement:** workers run focused checks; the orchestrator runs the project gate at integration. Full gates, calibration and mutation experiments are poor per-edit feedback when the question can be answered by one concrete witness
- **Semantic ownership:** task records and held contracts can be perfectly current while the chosen proxy misses the goal. Someone must inspect whether the evidence answers the original question, especially for art, content and play quality

Record command/rework count and elapsed check/review time for the chosen case if available, with concurrent work identified. Use that cost account to find friction; neither ceremony volume nor an unpaired duration demonstrates benefit.

## Bounded next steps

1. **Review the goal and evidence together.** Use one existing feature slice with behavior, content and visual acceptance. Keep the original goal, exact acceptance questions and final source/artifact revisions together in the owner's review package. Reuse retained private evidence rather than copying private materials into this public repository
2. **Trace one real correction.** Identify the failing observation, who chose the check, what BlaBla actually refused or preserved, the resulting edit, the rerun and the outcome. Also retain the self-hosting green-proxy counterexample above. Do not retrofit causal credit to the expert layer
3. **Inspect the result.** A reviewer checks the actual journey and rendered artifacts against the goal. Missing play, balance, long-run or device evidence remains open. A successful check cannot stand in for an unavailable observation
4. **Verify the approved remedy and bound further work.** Check the goal-guidance correction against its regression and semantic limits. Any additional goal-acceptance store or ceremony change requires a separate proposal and approval. No new paid model experiment or broad rerun is authorized by this plan
5. **Make the release decision.** Apply the acceptance below to that bounded account, then let the owner decide whether the candidate's demonstrated scope is worth releasing

### Future code options requiring approval

- **Only if needed: add an explicit, revision-bound owner/reviewer outcome acceptance record.** This could preserve the nonformal decision and prevent reuse after relevant work changes. It needs a separate design for authority, evidence references, freshness and revocation. It must not pretend to automate taste, force every goal into `OVERALL` or silently change existing completion semantics
- **Only if measured: simplify check-dependency updates or batch handoff mechanics.** Any additive input operation or command grouping must preserve exact-check identity, new acceptance epochs, current lenses and challenge prerequisites. Fewer invocations are useful only if they reduce actual friction without hiding a changed obligation

These options are proposals, not implemented 0.10 capabilities. The approved guidance correction above is intentionally smaller: it gives an honest next action, but cannot enforce that the review happened. Further machinery should wait for evidence of need and separate approval.

## Acceptance for a future 0.10 release

The product-readiness HOLD can be reconsidered after one finite review account establishes:

- **Purpose:** the owner’s original goal and a player/user-visible success criterion are clear; behavior, content and quality are all represented in the selected slice
- **Outcome:** actual observations identify what changed and what remains unaccepted, including one retained miss/correction and inspected rendered output. A test total, file count or task closure is not the outcome
- **Trust:** exact checks, declared dependencies, relevant revisions and review are identified. Human prompting, ordinary checks, BlaBla safeguards and optional expert advice receive separate attribution
- **Proxy failure:** the self-hosting goal mismatch is explicitly reviewed. The approved guidance remedy is verified, and any remaining acceptance limitation has an explicit owner decision and narrowly stated release scope. It must not disappear under a green aggregate
- **Cost:** the account identifies avoidable task/check/review churn and uses scoped verification. Do not claim savings without a matched comparison
- **Engineering and authority:** applicable final-revision checks and package scope are reported accurately, and the owner explicitly accepts release. Current Linux package and Windows/Ubuntu CI receipts are in [release evidence](0.10-release-evidence.md)

Stop this review when those items have an explicit accepted/open decision with evidence. If a missing observation needs new work or authorization, report that precise gap rather than inventing a larger experiment. A limited release may document an accepted limitation; a stronger general product claim must wait for stronger evidence.

Expert qualification remains separate: repository bindings are shadow-only, zero judgments are promoted, and real Kev calibration found no feasible policy. No useful live expert benefit is established. This plan does not reopen that holdout, authorize another model run, enable advisory delivery, or authorize merging, tagging, publishing or deploying.

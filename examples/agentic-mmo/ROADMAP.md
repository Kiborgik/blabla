# Rolling development horizon

Updated: 2026-10-16 09:00 UTC. Current planning window: through at least 2026-10-16 21:00 UTC.

## How this queue is used

The Creative Director maintains the intended outcomes and their order. The orchestrator opens bounded BlaBla tasks, owns shared interfaces and release activation, assigns disjoint scopes, and verifies integration. Agents keep their primary roles; when a role has no immediate work, the orchestrator assigns a small ready feature rather than leaving it idle or inventing unrelated cleanup.

This is a rolling twelve-hour lookahead, not a promise that all listed outcomes land within twelve hours. Replenish it as work is taken or live evidence changes priorities. Ready design work can proceed while an implementation dependency is being finished. Mechanics can be verified in the real headless engine before the durable public host is ready, but that is not called live online play.

Ship a functioning stage, observe it, and keep developing consequential capabilities. Correctness and persistence defects block the affected release. Fun, balance, tuning and visual polish are learned in play and do not become an indefinite hold on forward progress.

The full horizon remains early humanity through Mars and beyond, with scientific, cultural, political, economic and military ways to succeed. Do not manufacture an entire future campaign to fill the queue. Release new powers into the current world where compatible; explicitly migrate or archive and restart when necessary.

## Implemented local increment: consensual trade

The first trade release uses one simple data-defined exchange recipe: food for materials, delivered on a shared strategic beat every four completed actor rounds while the agreement remains active. Proposals, responses, withdrawal and stopping are immediate tactical actions. Calendar ticks and viewer animation speed do not schedule delivery. Terms and upkeep come from canonical data. Do not add a general market order book, auction system or free-form negotiation language yet.

### Agreement lifecycle

1. The proposing civilization selects a partner and a supported recipe. The proposal records both identities, exact offered/requested cargo, construction payer, recurring upkeep, delivery cadence and an expiry eight rounds after creation. There may be only one pending offer per unordered civilization pair, including crossed offers. Creating an offer does not debit the recipient or activate shipments.
2. Only the named recipient can accept or decline. Acceptance checks that the proposal is still pending and unexpired, both endpoints exist, and the declared construction payment remains affordable. It atomically creates one agreed route and records the terms each actor accepted. A failed check changes no resources or agreement state.
3. Acceptance never delivers cargo immediately: the first due delivery is the next strictly later strategic beat. A pending offer can be withdrawn by its proposer. Unanswered offers expire. A duplicate acceptance cannot create another route or spend the construction cost again.
4. Only a due strategic-beat shipment checks the combined cargo and upkeep obligations before any debit. Either transfer the agreed exchange completely or transfer nothing. Failed supply leaves a visible reason. It does not take one partner’s goods while silently skipping the other’s.
5. Either participant may stop future shipments. Stopping does not retroactively undo earlier deliveries. A nonparticipant cannot accept, withdraw or close another pair’s agreement.
6. The first release permits one active agreement per unordered civilization pair, independently of last-shipment success. A blocked shipment does not silently revoke consent. Revised terms require a new explicit agreement; do not silently change a partner’s existing obligations when content data changes.

The shared implementation uses `propose-trade`, `respond-trade`, `withdraw-trade` and `stop-trade`, with separate `TradeOffer` and `TradeAgreement` records. Its first recipe exchanges four proposer food for three recipient materials. Each pays one energy per due shipment and earns two credits; the proposer pays six materials and eight credits once on acceptance. These are current tuning data, not balance claims. Compact observations must expose incoming offers, accepted terms and the next due round. A future recipe edit cannot rewrite a stored agreement.

### What the map communicates

A pending proposal has no moving cargo. An active route shows traffic derived from real deliveries. A blocked route identifies its blocked endpoint and cause. A closed agreement stays in history but has no ongoing delivery animation. Mobile observers can tap the same facts that desktop users inspect.

### Required focused scenarios

- A proposal alone leaves the recipient’s resources unchanged across production rounds.
- Only the intended recipient can accept; third-party, stale and expired responses are rejected without partial state changes.
- Acceptance spends the construction cost once and creates one route; neither acceptance nor an intervening tactical round delivers cargo.
- A due strategic beat attempts at most one delivery. Accepting on a beat waits for the next beat, and retrying the same operation cannot deliver twice.
- Crossed offers cannot bypass the one-pending-pair rule; eight-round expiry is deterministic and persisted.
- One supplied exchange conserves the traded commodity totals across the two partners; explicit transport consumption and commerce production are accounted for separately.
- Insufficient combined cargo/upkeep at either endpoint produces no partial exchange and names the reason.
- Either participant can stop later shipments; a third party cannot.
- Restart preserves proposals, terms, active state and delivery history.
- An actual development-agent pair proposes and accepts through the same supported interface used by the service. A scripted test does not satisfy this play session.

The local implementation now rejects the legacy unilateral trade action and ships only accepted agreements. Actual content now makes `roads-between-peoples` require `first-harvest`, matching the trade-first sequence. Do not repeat these as unimplemented changes. Activation still needs the integration-owned release, current independent evidence and online read-back. The genuine pair play session remains required evidence of use; its outcome need not prove fun or balance.

## Next core specification: useful, conserved founding

Scope the next implementation around one immediate decision with a lasting geographical consequence. Activate `settlement-expansion` only when the following behavior is implemented and verified; the current gated population-creating path is not releasable as-is.

- The action names an owned source city and a legal destination tile. Move the canonical founding population of three from that source, leaving at least three people there. The source must therefore have at least six. Keep the existing canonical food 10, materials 12 and credits 8 cost unless a separately recorded data decision changes it.
- Check ownership, population, available supplies, destination occupation, allowed terrain and existing reach/separation rules before mutation. A failed or stale order spends nothing and moves nobody. A successful order conserves total civilization population across founding; ordinary subsequent growth is accounted for separately.
- Preserve the current world's tile IDs, terrain, cities, buildings, resources, clock and history on compatible application. No map regeneration or silent reroll. Old releases retain their old production until the new capability is explicitly applied.
- Add one canonical terrain-output table behind the feature: grassland gains two food per city production calculation; forest gains two materials; other terrain adds zero for this increment. Apply these through the existing productivity calculation, separately from the existing site-resource bonus. These are initial data values for real play. Observations explain both sources rather than pretending the raw bonus is the final rounded yield.
- Legal site choices expose terrain benefit, source people moved and stock cost. This makes a food site, a material site and retaining a concentrated capital understandable alternatives. Do not require every seeded start to have every option, and do not add random site bonuses to force variety.
- The new city uses the existing settlement defaults. Keep one civilization-wide stockpool for this increment; transport between cities is not silently simulated. A separate follow-on introduces city-targeted focus and construction before calling the new cities fully specialized.

Focused evidence: source and destination population conservation; rejected foreign or too-small source; atomic insufficient-cost failure; duplicate-site rejection; deterministic terrain output and release gating; no identity or clock change on feature application; supported observation exposes the true site choice. A real agent founding is a separate play observation, not a replacement for those checks.

The Art Director's visible moment is people leaving a named home, a small new settlement appearing on the chosen terrain, and a simple explanation such as "3 people moved from Dawnwell. Forest adds materials." City inspection must identify the selected city, not silently return to its capital. An animation is presentation of the committed move and never owns its timing.

Use the existing settlement/production responsibility and cohesive contract where that remains understandable. Current opening direction:

- If meaningful actor turns have not occurred, use a quiet season and let the civilizations establish priorities.
- Once production runs, prefer a resource discovery tied to an actual material shortage or workshop opportunity. Observe the resulting investment choice.
- Introduce drought when it exposes a real food or supply decision. The first such event is now verified at Aster, revision 20. Inspect its next action and following production before another pressure event. It should teach us about the world, not be a repeated random penalty.

The ten-minute event pass and hourly review add zero calendar years by default. An exceptional deliberate historical advance needs a stated feature/event cause, reason and amount, a stable deduplication identity, and verified before/after year. It is separate from the event effect and regular clock. Do not duplicate an opening quiet season as both the sixth-seat action and a scheduled pulse in the same interval. Unsupported events stay in the roadmap until their mechanics exist.

## Shared tactical and strategic pacing

Use fast actor choices across economy, research, trade, culture, politics and conflict. Keep their long commitments visible: project completion, scheduled cargo, affiliation, shared contributions, mobilization and reconstruction. The next trade increment introduces a common four-round strategic beat; other future systems should reuse it when suitable rather than each inventing an unrelated timer. Existing settlement yields remain per round until their own change is specified. Four and eight rounds are provisional data values to test in ongoing play, not a balance gate.

The observer should see both "what just happened" and "what is due next." Agents should receive the same due facts compactly. The six-minute historical calendar is not a six-minute delay between tactical actions; cinematic playback never changes either cadence.

## Keeping the horizon ahead

At each feature hand-back or meaningful live observation, the Creative Director updates what changed, which item is now ready, which dependency or assumption moved, and what should be explored next. Extend the horizon before it falls below twelve hours of plausible upcoming work; do not wait for the queue to empty.

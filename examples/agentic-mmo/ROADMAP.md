## Implemented local increment: consensual trade

The first trade release uses one simple data-defined exchange recipe: food for materials, delivered every four completed economy beats while the agreement remains active. The current local sequential implementation counts actor cycles; the concurrent successor uses shared timed economy beats. Proposals, responses, withdrawal and stopping are immediate tactical actions. Calendar ticks and viewer animation speed do not schedule delivery. Terms and upkeep come from canonical data. Do not add a general market order book, auction system or free-form negotiation language yet.

### Agreement lifecycle

1. The proposing civilization selects a partner and a supported recipe. The proposal records both identities, exact offered/requested cargo, construction payer, recurring upkeep, delivery cadence and an expiry eight rounds after creation. There may be only one pending offer per unordered civilization pair, including crossed offers. Creating an offer does not debit the recipient or activate shipments.
2. Only the named recipient can accept or decline. Acceptance checks that the proposal is still pending and unexpired, both endpoints exist, and the declared construction payment remains affordable. It atomically creates one agreed route and records the terms each actor accepted. A failed check changes no resources or agreement state.
3. Acceptance never delivers cargo immediately: the first due delivery is the next strictly later strategic beat. A pending offer can be withdrawn by its proposer. Unanswered offers expire. A duplicate acceptance cannot create another route or spend the construction cost again.
4. Only a due strategic-beat shipment checks the combined cargo and upkeep obligations before any debit. Either transfer the agreed exchange completely or transfer nothing. Failed supply leaves a visible reason. It does not take one partner’s goods while silently skipping the other’s.
5. Either participant may stop future shipments. Stopping does not retroactively undo earlier deliveries. A nonparticipant cannot accept, withdraw or close another pair’s agreement.
6. The first release permits one active agreement per unordered civilization pair, independently of last-shipment success. A blocked shipment does not silently revoke consent. Revised terms require a new explicit agreement; do not silently change a partner’s existing obligations when content data changes.

The shared implementation uses `propose-trade`, `respond-trade`, `withdraw-trade` and `stop-trade`, with separate `TradeOffer` and `TradeAgreement` records. Its first recipe exchanges four proposer food for three recipient materials. Each pays one energy per due shipment and earns two credits; the proposer pays six materials and eight credits once on acceptance. These are current tuning data, not balance claims. Compact observations must expose incoming offers, accepted terms and the next due round. A future recipe edit cannot rewrite a stored agreement.

### What the map communicates

A pending proposal has no moving cargo. An active route shows traffic derived from real deliveries. A blocked route identifies its blocked endpoint and cause. A closed agreement stays in history but has no ongoing delivery animation. Mobile observers can tap the same facts that desktop users inspect.

### Required focused scenarios

- A proposal alone leaves the recipient’s resources unchanged across production rounds.
- Only the intended recipient can accept; third-party, stale and expired responses are rejected without partial state changes.
- Acceptance spends the construction cost once and creates one route; neither acceptance nor an intervening tactical round delivers cargo.
- A due strategic beat attempts at most one delivery. Accepting on a beat waits for the next beat, and retrying the same operation cannot deliver twice.
- Crossed offers cannot bypass the one-pending-pair rule; eight-round expiry is deterministic and persisted.
- One supplied exchange conserves the traded commodity totals across the two partners; explicit transport consumption and commerce production are accounted for separately.
- Insufficient combined cargo/upkeep at either endpoint produces no partial exchange and names the reason.
- Either participant can stop later shipments; a third party cannot.
- Restart preserves proposals, terms, active state and delivery history.
- An actual development-agent pair proposes and accepts through the same supported interface used by the service. A scripted test does not satisfy this play session.

## Shared tactical and strategic pacing

The concurrent successor has two explicit server-time schedules, separate from the six-minute calendar and from animation:

- Tactical: each of the five civilizations may submit one accepted command in each ten-second server window. Different actors use the same window independently. Unused allowance does not accumulate. - Strategic: one economy beat becomes due every sixty seconds. Standing city focus, research and supply resolve without any all-agent quorum. Trade is due every fourth completed economy beat, with the first shipment strictly later than acceptance. An idle actor's standing choices continue; it cannot freeze the world.
- Director: supported scheduled event operations operate independently. There is no mandatory sixth turn before production. Director events and historical clock ticks retain their own durable IDs and default zero extra years.
